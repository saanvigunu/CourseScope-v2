Special Topics in Information

Sociocultural AI
Naitian Zhou
Jan 19, 2027 - May 07, 2027
Tu
02:00 pm - 04:59 pm
South Hall 205
Class #:17282
Units:3

Instruction Mode: In-Person Instruction

No Open Seats
This course explores modern AI technologies (transformers, LLMs, speech and vision language models) as cultural ones that are trained on, mediate, and increasingly participate in our social and cultural lives. Accounts of phenomena like AI slop demand not only a thorough technical understanding of the mechanism through which large language models generate text, but also lucid theoretical frameworks of reader reception and language ideology. In this course, we ask how we might use these technologies to construct computational measures of cultural concepts, in order to advance our humanistic and social scientific understanding of the world. But, on equal footing, we explore how we can apply theories of language, culture and communication to gain a better understanding of the ways in which they draw on and interact with our sociocultural world. This course is designed for an interdisciplinary audience; students from information science, humanities and social sciences, and computer science are all welcome. Each class will cover the technical foundations of how language technologies work (e.g., tokenization, sequence-to-sequence models, diffusion) as well as contextualize it within an understanding of culture as emergent through social interaction.
