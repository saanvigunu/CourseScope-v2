This graduate course examines the foundations and applications of augmented reality (AR), virtual reality (VR), and immersive computing. Students connect human perception with the hardware, computer vision, and interaction techniques that support immersive experiences, while learning to evaluate existing technologies and develop solutions that address users’ needs. Topics include visual and multisensory perception, 3D displays, depth cameras, perspective projection, marker-based tracking, and structure from motion. The course also explores 3D input devices, speech and language interfaces, user orientation and movement, virtual identity, and inclusive design, alongside developments in artificial intelligence and hardware acceleration. Programming assignments provide experience with Unity3D, AR/VR application development, smartphone deployment, and computer vision. A final project brings together background research, prototyping, progress reviews, presentations, and a written report. By the end of the course, students should be able to critically assess AR/VR hardware and software and design and implement immersive applications informed by technical capabilities and human needs. Students should have programming proficiency in C, C++, or C#, with preparation comparable to CS 61A and CS 61B.

Performance Analysis and Optimization of Computer Systems
Rishabh Iyer
Jan 19, 2027 - May 07, 2027
Mo, We
05:00 pm - 06:29 pm
The Gateway Building B1019
Class #:34207
Units:4

Instruction Mode: In-Person Instruction

Open Seats

This graduate-level course covers principles and techniques for analyzing and improving the performance of computer systems. The course begins with classical topics such as caching, load balancing, and scheduling, which have long guided resource management and performance optimization. It then transitions to modern challenges, examining techniques that enable efficiency and performance predictability in today’s large-scale, heterogeneous environments. Finally, it surveys how ideas from machine learning and programming languages can contribute novel insights and tools for system performance analysis. The course will cover a diverse range of systems---including operating systems, distributed databases, data analytics frameworks, and machine learning systems such as LLM-serving platforms---to enable a holistic understanding of how to reason about and improve the performance of networked systems in practice.
